What happens at exactly 100%, and on the 15th of the month

Two edge cases the engine has to get precisely right: the atomic hard stop, and a seat added mid-cycle.

0

A hard stop is a door that locks at exactly 100%, not before or after

Reaching the limit is fine; a single credit past it is refused entirely, never partially let through.

1

Alice's $10 ULB, hit to the penny

The event that lands exactly on the cap is fully accepted — 100% is a valid, allowed state, not a violation.

2

One more credit ($0.01) is refused in full, not trimmed to $0

This is what "atomic" means in practice: the engine never charges $0.00 worth and lets the rest through — the full request is one unit.

3

A seat added on day 15 doesn't cost a full month

The engine counts calendar days from the assignment date through month end — day 15 itself counts, giving 16 billable days in a 30-day month.

4

The exact formula behind that 16/30 slice

Both the seat's dollar charge and its included-credit contribution scale by the same day fraction — a late-cycle seat gets less of both.

5

But leaving mid-cycle doesn't hand any of it back

Removing a seat only changes when access stops — the credits it already contributed to the pool are never clawed back mid-cycle.

Precision at the edges is the whole job: exactly 100% is allowed, one cent past it is not, and every day of a seat's life is counted, not rounded.

Gotcha: GitHub's docs say prorated credits "may" apply without publishing the exact formula, so the simulator also offers a full-credit hypothetical purely for comparison — it is explicitly not the documented default.
